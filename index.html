Where the money goes

Four losses that never appear on a bill

None of them look dramatic. That is exactly why they get away with it, month after month.

Loss 01

The missed enquiry

Messaged at 9 PM, saw your reply at 11 AM, had already booked somewhere else. You never even knew he existed.

Loss 02

The vanishing regular

Came every month for two years, then stopped. Nobody noticed for five months, and by then he was somebody else's regular.

Loss 03

The ageing payment

Sixty days old. You keep meaning to call. It is awkward, so it waits, and every week it gets harder to ask.

Loss 04

The quiet Tuesday

Half your capacity empty at 2 PM while a full Saturday turns customers away. Nobody moved one to the other.

Scope of work

Six departments. One employee.

A human hire sits in one function. GHOST is taught the whole business — so the enquiry, the booking, the bill, the pending amount and the follow-up are one story instead of five disconnected ones.

Onboarding

How to hire him: four steps, two weeks

It is an induction, not an installation. A new employee needs a fortnight to be useful, and this one is honest about that.

01Day 1

You describe the business

A conversation, not a form full of settings. What you sell, your timings, your staff, and the three things that keep going wrong.

02Days 2–5

We write his job description

In writing, before anything goes live: which jobs in which departments he handles, and what stays with you. Approve it or change it.

03Week 2

He works under your review

In week two nothing goes out unseen. He drafts, you approve, he learns from your corrections. Then you let him run.

04Every month

He reports every month

On the first of each month, a written account of what he handled and what it was worth. If the number is poor, you see that too.

Stated limits

What he cannot do

Every employee has limits, and ours are written down before you hire. The fastest way to lose your trust is to let you assume something we never promised.

No professional advice

No medical, legal or financial opinions to your customers, under any wording. He answers what you sell and what it costs, and routes the rest to you.

He cannot commit for you

No discounts invented, no deadlines promised, no exceptions granted. Anything outside what you set becomes a message asking you.

He only knows what he is told

He cannot chase a payment nobody recorded. Someone still has to enter the bill. The onboarding sets up that one habit, and we are direct about it.

He does not replace your team

He has no judgement and cannot read a room. He removes the work your staff should never have been doing, so they can do the part that needs a person.

He gets things wrong at first

Every new employee does. That is what the review week is for, and why corrections in month one are expected rather than a fault.

He never shares your data

Your customer list is yours. It is not pooled with other businesses, and it is not used to sell anything to anyone else.

Questions owners actually ask

Before you hire him

Do I need to be good with computers?

No. If you can use WhatsApp, you can work with GHOST. There is no dashboard, no software to install, and nothing for your staff to be trained on. You talk to him the way you talk to a person who works for you.

Do I have to switch on all six departments?

No, and most owners do not. Start with the one or two jobs costing you the most — usually enquiries or pending payments. Because it is the same employee throughout, switching on marketing or staff later does not mean starting again. He already knows your business.

My business has jobs specific to my trade. Can he learn those?

That is the normal case. The six departments are a starting structure, not a fixed list. If a job is repeatable and you can describe it in a sentence, it can usually be written into his job description during the onboarding.

Will my customers know they are talking to a machine?

We do not pretend otherwise, and we would advise you not to either. In practice customers care about a straight answer quickly. He introduces himself as your business, answers what he knows, and hands over to a person the moment the question needs one.

What happens to my existing WhatsApp number?

It keeps working. He operates on your business number, so the number on your board and your conversation history stay exactly as they are. We walk you through the change during setup.

Can he handle the way my customers actually write?

That is the normal case, not the exception. Customers write in short forms, in a mix of English and their own language, often without punctuation. He is built for how people really message, not for clean sentences.

What does he cost?

He is priced like a salary — one monthly amount for one employee, with no separate charge per task or per department. The figure follows the scope you choose, and the three scopes are set out above. We quote after the first conversation rather than putting a number here that may not apply to your business.

What if he is not working for me?

Tell us in the first month and we will either fix the job description or stop. We would rather lose one customer early than have a business paying for an employee it does not rate.
